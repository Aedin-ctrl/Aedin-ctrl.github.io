Every crypto up/down window on Kalshi, Polymarket and Gemini — the whole path of each one, recorded every few seconds, and how it actually settled. Riding on top: a paper test of one rule, with $100 of play money per venue. No real money is involved anywhere in this page.

The rule being tested

Wait until the last fifth of the window — minute 4 of 5, minute 12 of 15. Look at which way the coin is moving right then. Buy that side, cross the spread, pay the venue's real fee, hold to settlement. One contract a time.

What it actually buys, which was not what I expected. The prediction here was that the rule would pay 80–90¢ for the side already winning. It does the opposite: in the first hour, 22 of 23 trades bought the underdog, at an average of 24¢. Because "trending" means the coin's direction of travel and not "is it above the window's open", the rule mostly fires when the price rose early and is now drifting back — so it buys the cheap side, betting the drift continues. That is a bet on short-term reversal against the window's position, which is a different and more interesting thing than backing the favourite.

It still has to clear a real cost: the spread on these venues gets wider near the close, not tighter, and fills here cross it and pay the venue's fee. Prices below 2¢ and above 97¢ are refused — at those ends the market is saying the window is already over, and a 0.1¢ contract that happens to win pays a thousandfold and would swamp every other trade in the record.

Does the way it is going tell you how it finishes? — the question underneath all of this

For each point through a window, how often the direction of travel at that moment matched the finish. Read the grey line first. It is a random walk with this coin's own volatility and no predictability whatsoever — and it still scores about 65%, because the last fifth of a window is part of the window. Only the gap between the two lines means anything.

ROI is profit over what was actually risked — the honest denominator for a contract bought at 85¢. Hit is how often the side bought was the side that won.

Why trades were not taken — the denominator

A strategy that logs only its fills reports the average of the trades it was able to get, which is a different and much more flattering number. Every refusal is recorded with its reason.

Can you even buy it? — offers by how far through the window

How often each side has an offer resting on it, by tenth of the window (up / down, as percentages). Offers thin out sharply towards the close, and once a window is effectively decided — the favourite bid at 99¢ — its ask disappears completely: you can buy the loser for a penny, but the side the rule wants cannot be bought at any price. Measured at 73 seconds left on a window that was not yet decided, the favourite still had 509 contracts offered at 97¢, so this is about the decided case specifically, not a blanket rule. Either way the rule is unbuyable exactly when it is most confident, which is the refusal that shows up most often below.

What exists to trade — checked against all three venues

No venue runs an hourly or daily up/down market any more. Kalshi and Gemini list strike ladders at those lengths ("BTC $64,400 or above"), which is a different contract; Polymarket's hourly and daily up/down windows were discontinued after 2025. So the paper accounts trade 5- and 15-minute windows, and the longer timeframes are studied from the price record instead — which needs no contract.

Paper only — there is no order path in this system and no venue credential with write scope. Collected on the Pi every 15 seconds. Prices come from each venue's real order book, never from a summary field: Polymarket's cached summary was measured 22¢ away from its own book, so quoting it would manufacture profit that does not exist.
