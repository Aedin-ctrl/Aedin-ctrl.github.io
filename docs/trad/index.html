PART 2

How the thing actually works

four moving parts, on real gold

Everything below is computed in your browser from COMEX gold futures bars, so you can move the parameters and watch the stop, the trades and the money change. Nothing here is fitted for you.

The recurrence

One line does all of it. Each bar, the stop closes a fixed fraction of its remaining distance to the best price the trend has reached — and that fraction grows every time the trend makes a new extreme, so the stop accelerates into price the longer the run lasts.

SARnext  =  SAR  +  AF × ( EP − SAR )
EP — extreme point. The highest high since the trend began (long), or the lowest low (short). Never moves against the trend; resets on every flip.
AF — acceleration factor. Starts at step and adds another step every time EP makes a new extreme — counting new highs, not bars — capped at max. A trend that goes sideways does not accelerate.
Clamp — the stop may never sit inside the last two bars' range. Without it the stop could start a bar already triggered, and the system would flip on every sharp move.
Flip — when price trades through the stop, the position reverses: the new stop becomes the old trend's EP, the new EP is this bar's extreme, and AF resets to step.

Cost ladder · where the venue eats it

The micro is not the big contract divided by ten. The spread is quoted in index points and so is the price, so half a tick costs the same 0.16 bps on MES as on ES. Commission is quoted in dollars per contract, so it is divided by a notional ten times smaller — 0.20 bps on MES against 0.06 bps on ES. That is the entire cost difference between them.
