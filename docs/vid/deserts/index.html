WHY DO THE WORLD'S DESERTS
SIT AT THE SAME LATITUDE

Nearly every big hot desert on Earth is in one of two bands about thirty degrees from the equator. Air rises at the equator, cools, drops its water as rainforest, spreads polewards at ten kilometres up at around minus fifty -- and comes back down around thirty degrees, warming as it is compressed. From minus fifty to plus thirty the amount of water that air could carry goes up about seven hundredfold, and it picked none up on the way down.
