Why a train wheel stays on the rail

Episode #047 rebuilt in the mini style. Every claim the narration makes has a thing in the scene that proves it — a bar whose length is the quantity, a ghost of where it started, an arrow that moves with the part. The words describe the picture; they are never the only place the point exists.

0.0 / 24.0s

Nothing here is asserted. Every number is built on screen: the cone is two bars from one line with the difference in green; the shift is a gap against a ghost; the two radii are measured up from the rail, the one line both wheels genuinely touch, and then stood side by side; and the final distance is one turn of band wrapped around each of those two circles and unrolled along the rail it runs on. If you cannot see where a quantity came from, the beat is not finished. (The band sits 13 mm proud of the wheel, so each band is that much longer than its wheel's own circumference — the difference between them, which is the thing being compared, is untouched.)

Declared exaggerations. The cone is drawn about 12× real (a real tread is coned 1 in 20) and the tread is widened to 200 mm so the slope reads without turning the wheel into a pulley. The sideways shift is 70 mm; the real flangeway is drawn at 30 mm rather than 10, because the flangeway is the only thing that limits the shift at all. The flanges still retract on beat 3 — at 90 mm even the widened flange would be inside the rail head, so the piece takes them off and says so rather than quietly letting two solids share the same space. The spoken numbers are the true ones.
