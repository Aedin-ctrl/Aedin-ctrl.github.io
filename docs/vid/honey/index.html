CATHODE DESK

House style v2.4 — one medium, not four. A 16-bit program, running in a Win95 window, drawing voxel diagrams, photographed off a green-tinted CRT. Every frame passes through all four layers in order, every time. Sampler content: why sealed honey doesn't spoil.

Press AUDIO ON for the voice, the effects and the bed — browsers will not start sound without a click, so it plays silent until you do.

Everything but the voice is synthesised in the browser from the same score the picture uses, so it seeks instantly and stays a pure function of t. window.scoreSound(a,b) returns the events in a window — that is what an offline render would mix from. The voice is one 59.6s track: nineteen sentences rendered separately and placed on a uniform grid — exactly 0.45s between sentences, 1.20s between beats, zero irregular. Word onsets come from transcribing the rendered audio back with whisper.cpp, so on-screen words appear as they are spoken.

NUMBERS USED IN THE SAMPLER

aw 0.60

Honey's water activity. Typical range 0.55–0.65.

not the same as water content (~17%)

aw 0.91

The floor most bacteria need to grow. Below it they lose water to the surroundings faster than they can take it in.

osmosis

pH 3.9

Typical honey; the range across honeys is about 3.4–6.1. Compared on screen to orange juice (3.3–4.2), not a tomato — a tomato is 4.3–4.9, so honey is the more acidic of the two.

1 cube = 1 pH unit

~19% moisture

Above roughly this, honey ferments — osmotolerant yeasts (Zygosaccharomyces) grow down to about aw 0.61, just above honey's 0.60. And honey is hygroscopic: left open it pulls water out of the air until it gets there.

why the seal matters

Not peroxide

Glucose oxidase is inactive in ripened, undiluted honey and only regains activity on dilution. The peroxide story explains honey on a wound, not honey in a sealed jar — it was in earlier cuts of this sampler and has been removed.

corrected 2026-11-08
Legibility floor. Video gets watched on a phone. At 1280×720: headlines ≥44px, any label the story depends on ≥28px, key numbers ≥64px. In the 640×360 buffer that is ≥22, ≥14, ≥32.

Determinism. Every frame is a pure function of t — all randomness from H.rng(seed) inside the draw, never Math.random. Same t, same pixels, so the frame-by-frame video renderer works.

Checked, not eyeballed. checkBounds(), checkOverlap() and checkLegibility() and checkStraddle() sweep every frame with a recorder on every piece of text drawn — and on every sprite, voxel stack and tag plate text could hide behind. They report anything leaving the client area, landing on top of something else, or set below the floor. All six return 0 for this build — truncation, clearance, straddle, overlap, bounds and the type floor — including the one that catches a label lying across the group box's border, which the panel check structurally cannot see; the previous one had 21 texts under the floor, including the opening question at 9px.

Two ticks, not one. Drawn content steps at 12fps — sprites, dither, typed text, the blink. Motion isn't ticked at all: the cursor, the voxel drops, the arrows and the counters run at the display's own rate. A 24fps tick was tried first and the cubes still stepped — and a cube that steps reads as a dropped frame, not as a style. The pixel grid is held by the ×2 nearest-neighbour blit, not by quantising time.

50.4 seconds, 35.7 of it speech. The picture is hung off the voice, not off arithmetic. Each beat is one audio file, every sentence is written to end on the word that triggers its visual, and the sentence boundaries are read back from the TTS — then the real end of each sentence's speech is measured off the waveform, because the reported boundary runs to the next sentence and so includes the pause after the last word. 0.60 finishes counting on the word “six”; 0.91 on “one”; the 7 on “seven”. One timeline table feeds the picture, the effects and the narration placement, so they cannot drift apart.
