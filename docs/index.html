MemeMatch

Make a face at your webcam and it tells you which emoji or hamster meme you are, with a matching mask on you and on it. It reads your expression and which fingers are out, and you can train it on your own face in about a minute. Everything runs on your device.

10
DoodleLife

Draw something and it comes alive as what it is, using your own wobbly lines: fish swim, birds flap, cats walk on their legs, cars drive, flowers sway. They chase each other, eat food you scribble, and night falls when you draw a moon. A neural network recognises the doodle right on your device.

JavaScript · DoodleNet (Quick, Draw!) in plain JS · Canvas — 2026

11
Gatecraft

Wire up logic gates and watch them work. Drop switches, gates and lamps on the board, drag between pins to connect them, and it simulates live — wires light up when they carry a 1. It works out the truth table for you, so you can check a circuit does what you think. Latches and flip-flops hold their state properly, because it settles the circuit the way real gates do. A whole design saves into the link, so sharing one is sharing a URL.

JavaScript · Inline SVG · Settling logic simulator — 2026

12

A standing set of twenty alternative designs for this site, each a self-contained page with its own layout and its own one idea — a scoring box, an oscilloscope, a lab notebook. New ones are added in batches and the weakest are retired, so the set turns over. Every page is checked for contrast, keyboard use, phone layout in Safari as well as Chrome, and whether it actually stops moving when you ask it to.

HTML · CSS · No framework · Playwright + axe gates — 2026

13

Games built from scratch — the engine, the simulation, the sound, and the tools that prove it works. Each has a design document recording what broke and the check that now prevents it.

Privacy

What this site, LectureNotes and typr do with your information. Short version: almost nothing.

Last updated September 28, 2026

This website

  • No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing on this site stores anything in your browser or counts your visits, so there's nothing to consent to.
  • No accounts or forms. The address bar at the top only navigates this page; nothing you type is sent anywhere.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security and operations; see GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Fonts are served from this site, not from Google.
  • Embedded projects. Some projects open inside the page from my other sites (casino.aedinlai.com, calorietracker.aedinlai.com, hallway-marathon.github.io). Those pages load fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives your IP address when you open them.
  • News. The news tab's question box opens Claude (claude.ai) in a new tab with your question and the story; nothing is sent until you press Ask, and then it goes to Anthropic under your own Claude account. Source links go to the publishers' sites.
  • Email. If you email me, I get your address and whatever you write, and use it only to reply.

LectureNotes (Mac and Windows app)

  • I don't collect anything. The app sends no analytics or usage data to me, and I never see your recordings, transcripts or notes.
  • Stays on your computer: audio is transcribed on your computer and never uploaded. Transcripts, answers and notes are saved to ~/Documents/LectureNotes (on Windows, Documents\LectureNotes). Answers from the free on-device AI model are also produced on your computer. A small log of when Claude calls succeeded or failed is kept in ~/Library/Logs/LectureNotes (on Windows, %LOCALAPPDATA%\LectureNotes\Logs, next to a troubleshooting log of app errors that also never leaves your PC).
  • If you connect Claude (optional), the questions it detects (with the few sentences said just before them), screenshots you take with the camera tool, and the transcript used to write notes are sent to Anthropic through your own Claude account, to produce the answers. Anthropic's privacy policy and your plan's terms apply to that.
  • Installing it downloads files from this site, existential.audio (BlackHole audio driver, Mac only), ollama.com (Ollama and the AI model) and huggingface.co (the speech model). Those servers see your IP address, like any download.

typr (the Mac app)

  • I don't collect anything. No analytics or usage data, and I never see your scripts.
  • Stays on your Mac: your script, settings and the draft wording it generates (saved in ~/Library/Application Support/typr).
  • Keyboard access (the Accessibility permission) is used only to type into the document in front of you and, during a take, to notice when a key is pressed or the mouse is clicked so it can pause. Keys and clicks aren't recorded or saved.
  • If Claude Code is signed in on your Mac, the script is sent to Anthropic through your own Claude account to write the rough-draft wording. Anthropic's privacy policy and your plan's terms apply to that.
  • Installing it downloads the app from this site.
