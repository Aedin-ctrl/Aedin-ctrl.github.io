01 LectureNotes Listens to a lecture, writes it down live, and answers questions asked out loud. macOS and Windows (beta).

A native macOS app that listens to a lecture and writes it down for you. It captures your mic and your computer's own audio at the same time, so a Zoom call comes out as a single speaker-tagged transcript, live, as it happens. When someone asks a question out loud it notices, answers it in a side tab on its own, and there's a camera tool for pointing at a slide and asking about it. Transcription always happens on your computer. Answers come from an on-device AI model, unless you connect your own Claude account; then questions, screenshots and notes are sent to Anthropic to answer them ().

C++ · Objective-C++ · whisper.cpp · Dear ImGui · Ollama — 2025

Paste this into Terminal. It installs the app, the audio driver it needs, and the models it runs on — all of it, in one go.

curl -fsSL https://www.aedinlai.com/install-lecturenotes.sh | bash

Apple Silicon Macs (M1 or newer), macOS Sonoma or later. Downloads about 4 GB of models, so give it time. It asks for your password once, to install the audio driver. Safe to re-run — it skips whatever you already have. You can read the script first, and you should.

On Windows (beta), paste this into PowerShell instead:

irm https://www.aedinlai.com/install-lecturenotes.ps1 | iex

Windows 10 (22H2) or 11, on Intel/AMD or ARM PCs. It builds the app on your PC from its source code into a LectureNotes App folder on your Desktop (the free build tools are about 240 MB, downloaded once), then sets up the same local AI and speech models as the Mac. No administrator password, and no audio driver: Windows records your computer's sound on its own. It's a beta and not code-signed yet, so on PCs with Smart App Control turned on, Windows may refuse to open it. You can read the script first.

Before you record: get permission. Some places, including Massachusetts, require everyone's consent to record a conversation, and most schools have their own rules about recording classes and outside help. You're responsible for following them. By installing you agree to the .

Have a Claude Pro or Max plan? Click Set up Claude in the app's first-launch checklist and it'll answer with Claude instead of the on-device model. One sign-in in your browser, using your own account. Without it, everything still works for free.

02 typr Fakes someone writing and revising an essay on screen, for filming. Types into any document. macOS.

A macOS app I made for a short film, to fake someone writing an essay on screen. Give it the final text (paste it, or drop in a PDF, Word doc or photo of the page) and a running time, and it types into whatever document is open — Google Docs, Word, Pages — at a human pace, with pauses, typos and second thoughts. Then it goes back through its own rough draft, jumping word by word with the keyboard, highlighting and rewording, until the page matches the script exactly. A floating bar shows the timer for each part. If anyone touches the keyboard it pauses instantly and remembers its place, or it can let an actor's keypresses drive the typing so their hands match the screen.

Swift · SwiftUI · AppKit · Claude — 2026

Paste this into Terminal. It installs the app, and nothing else.

curl -fsSL https://www.aedinlai.com/install-typr.sh | bash

macOS Ventura or later, Apple Silicon or Intel. About 1 MB. On first launch it asks for Accessibility permission, which is what lets it type into your document. Safe to re-run — it replaces an older copy. You can read the script first, and you should.

Have Claude Code signed in on your Mac? typr uses it to write the rough-draft wording it later revises; your script is sent to Anthropic through your own account to do that (). Without it, it uses a built-in word list — or open a .typr scene file someone made with Claude and you get exactly their takes.

Privacy

What this site, LectureNotes and typr do with your information. Short version: almost nothing.

Last updated September 28, 2026

This website

  • No cookies, no analytics, no tracking. Nothing on this site stores anything in your browser or counts your visits, so there's nothing to consent to.
  • No accounts or forms. The address bar at the top only navigates this page; nothing you type is sent anywhere.
  • Hosting. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub may log visitors' IP addresses for security and operations; see GitHub's privacy statement.
  • Fonts are served from this site, not from Google.
  • Embedded projects. Some projects open inside the page from my other sites (casino.aedinlai.com, calorietracker.aedinlai.com, hallway-marathon.github.io). Those pages load fonts from Google Fonts, so Google receives your IP address when you open them.
  • Email. If you email me, I get your address and whatever you write, and use it only to reply.

LectureNotes (Mac and Windows app)

  • I don't collect anything. The app sends no analytics or usage data to me, and I never see your recordings, transcripts or notes.
  • Stays on your computer: audio is transcribed on your computer and never uploaded. Transcripts, answers and notes are saved to ~/Documents/LectureNotes (on Windows, Documents\LectureNotes). Answers from the free on-device AI model are also produced on your computer. A small log of when Claude calls succeeded or failed is kept in ~/Library/Logs/LectureNotes (on Windows, %LOCALAPPDATA%\LectureNotes\Logs, next to a troubleshooting log of app errors that also never leaves your PC).
  • If you connect Claude (optional), the questions it detects (with the few sentences said just before them), screenshots you take with the camera tool, and the transcript used to write notes are sent to Anthropic through your own Claude account, to produce the answers. Anthropic's privacy policy and your plan's terms apply to that.
  • Installing it downloads files from this site, existential.audio (BlackHole audio driver, Mac only), ollama.com (Ollama and the AI model) and huggingface.co (the speech model). Those servers see your IP address, like any download.

typr (the Mac app)

  • I don't collect anything. No analytics or usage data, and I never see your scripts.
  • Stays on your Mac: your script, settings and the draft wording it generates (saved in ~/Library/Application Support/typr).
  • Keyboard access (the Accessibility permission) is used only to type into the document in front of you and, during a take, to notice when a key is pressed or the mouse is clicked so it can pause. Keys and clicks aren't recorded or saved.
  • If Claude Code is signed in on your Mac, the script is sent to Anthropic through your own Claude account to write the rough-draft wording. Anthropic's privacy policy and your plan's terms apply to that.
  • Installing it downloads the app from this site.
