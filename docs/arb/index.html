Calibration Board

What prediction markets were priced at, against what actually happened. Every figure uses the price you could have transacted at — the ask to buy, the bid to sell, never the midpoint — and only quotes with real depth behind them.

What has been collected

The collector runs on the Pi and keeps running with every laptop in the house shut. It records the order book for open markets, then records how each one settled.

Is the price right?

Each point is a band of prices. The diagonal is a perfectly calibrated market. A point above the line happened more often than it was priced for; below, less often. Bars are 95% confidence intervals — where they cross the line, the market is not measurably wrong.

Could you have traded it?

Gross is the mispricing. Net is after the Kalshi fee at a realistic order size — the fee ceiling is charged per order, so size matters a great deal. A row only counts as an edge if its confidence interval clears the price and net is positive.

Source calib.json, rebuilt every 5 minutes. Read-only: the collector holds no trading credentials and cannot place an order.
