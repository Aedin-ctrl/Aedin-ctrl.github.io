What the columns mean

Return is what the bet actually pays, after everything. Under each bet is the arithmetic behind it: the gap between the two venues' prices, less fees, less tax. In is what you put up across both venues and keep is what comes back, whichever way the event lands.

A positive gap is not enough on its own, which is why the board does not list one. Kalshi's fee is largest in the middle of the price range, so a 1% gap on a 30¢ contract loses money — the fee there is 1.47% of the payout and no amount of size fixes it. Only the tails are cheap enough for a thin gap to survive, which is why nearly everything here is a longshot.

Risk : win is the part most arbitrage maths leaves out. The two legs only cancel if both venues settle the same way. If they do not — a different season or scope than it looks, an oracle result that departs from the league's official one, a void on one venue only, a delisting — both legs lose and the entire stake in the “in” column goes, to win what is in “keep”. That ratio is the number worth looking at. It is an exposure, not a probability: nothing here can estimate how likely it is.

The fee is 0.07 × price × (1 − price) per contract on Kalshi: 1.75¢ at a coin flip, 0.33¢ at 5¢ or 95¢. Polymarket is modelled at no trading fee. Tax assumes both legs are exchange contracts that net against each other.
