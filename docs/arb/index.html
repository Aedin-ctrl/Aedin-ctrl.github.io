Before you act on one of these

Once both legs are filled you are safe — exactly one pays out, so the total is fixed and later price moves cannot touch it. That only holds if you hold to settlement, which for most rows here is mid-2027; selling early means selling at whatever the market is then.

Between the two fills you are not safe, and that is where the money is actually lost. There is no atomic order across two venues: you place one, then the other. Both quote in whole cents, so a single tick is 1% of the payout — larger than most of the edges on this board. If the second venue moves one cent while you are getting there, you either hold one naked leg, which is an outright bet on a team, or you complete at a worse price and lose more than the arbitrage was worth.

These rows are also short-lived: measured over the collector's whole log, 53% of them are gone within one poll and the median lifetime is under a minute. A card that has been standing for several polls is a far better prospect than one first seen this poll, and each card says which it is. None of this is priced into the figures above — they assume both legs fill at the quoted price, instantly.

Reading a card

The big number is what the bet returns after everything. Underneath each card is its working: the gap between the two venues, less fees, less tax.

A positive gap is not enough on its own. Kalshi's fee is 0.07 × price × (1 − price) per contract, biggest in the middle of the range — 1.75¢ at a coin flip against 0.33¢ at 5¢ or 95¢. So a 1% gap on a 30¢ contract loses money whatever size you trade, because the fee there is 1.47% of the payout. Only the tails are cheap enough for a thin gap to survive, which is why nearly every bet here is a longshot.

Risk is the ratio most arbitrage maths leaves out. The two legs only cancel if both venues settle the same way. If they do not — a different season or scope than it looks, an oracle result that departs from the league's official one, a void on one venue only, a delisting — both legs lose and the entire stake goes, to win the few dollars in “keep”. It is an exposure, not a probability: nothing here can estimate how likely it is.
